# Hypotheses

Every line here is a hypothesis about how a small, fast-growing London office *might* work. None is a claim about Terra. Each has a cheap way to check it and a result that would make me drop it.

| # | Hypothesis | How I would test it in week one | Kill signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Binding approval to an exact record fingerprint prevents "approved at £1,850, invoiced at £2,450" drift | Compare the last 10 office vendor approvals with their current terms | No differences found and no one remembers one |
| H2 | One critical step (e.g. bank call-back) gets skipped when the checklist is "mostly done" | Ask who verifies bank details and look at the last three onboardings | Every onboarding has every step evidenced |
| H3 | A brief with explicit denominators leads to fewer "is that good?" follow-ups in the meeting | Run two weeks with denominators, two without; count clarification questions | No change in questions, or founders ignore the KPI block |
| H4 | Owner follow-through rates are misread when n is small | Show rates with and without the minimum-sample hold; ask leaders what they conclude | Leaders already discount small samples unprompted |
| H5 | Decision requests with a named owner get decided in the meeting rather than carried over | Track carried-over decisions for four weeks | Carry-over rate is already near zero |
| H6 | A board export that refuses a stale brief stops last-minute edits leaking into board material unreviewed | Diff the last two board packs against the data they cite | Packs already match their sources |

The tool makes each test cheap: the audit export records every change with fingerprints, and the brief ID changes whenever its content does.
